Handmade Pasta Making from Scratch

Published by Aura Journal • August 2026

There is something quietly satisfying about turning a few everyday ingredients into fresh pasta. A little flour, eggs, time, and patient hands can become ribbons ready for a simple sauce or a relaxed meal around the table. The process invites you to slow down and enjoy the making as much as the eating.

Homemade pasta does not require a professional kitchen or a cupboard full of specialist equipment. A clean work surface, a fork, a rolling pin, and a sharp knife are enough to begin. A pasta machine can be useful, but it is an optional addition rather than a requirement.

Start with a Simple Ingredient List

A classic fresh egg pasta dough often begins with flour and eggs. Some cooks prefer finely milled “00” flour for its delicate texture, while all-purpose flour is a practical option for a first attempt. The amount of flour needed can vary with egg size, flour type, and the humidity in your kitchen, so treat measurements as a starting point rather than a rigid rule.

Make a mound of flour on a clean counter and create a well in the centre. Add the eggs to the well and gently beat them with a fork, gradually drawing flour inward from the edges. When the mixture becomes too thick for the fork, use your hands to bring the dough together.

Knead, Rest, and Roll

Kneading develops the dough's structure. Press it forward with the heel of your hand, fold it back, turn it, and repeat. At first, it may feel uneven or slightly sticky. Continue until it becomes smoother and more elastic, adding only small amounts of flour if it is sticking excessively.

Good pasta begins with patience: give the dough time to come together before asking it to become something else.

Wrap the dough or cover it well and let it rest at room temperature for around 30 minutes. Resting makes it easier to roll because the dough has time to relax. Keep it covered whenever you are not working with it so the surface does not dry out.

Divide the dough into manageable pieces. Flatten one piece, then roll it from the centre outward, turning it regularly to help maintain an even shape. Aim for a thin sheet that is easy to handle without tearing. Dust lightly with flour as needed, but avoid adding so much that the dough becomes dry.

Shape Pasta to Suit the Meal

Once rolled, the sheet can become tagliatelle, pappardelle, or simple squares for filled pasta. For ribbons, lightly dust the sheet, fold it loosely, and cut it into even strips with a sharp knife. Unfold the strands and arrange them in small loose nests on a floured surface.

Do not worry if the pieces are not perfectly identical. Handmade pasta has a natural variation that is part of its appeal. Consistent thickness matters more than perfect edges because it helps the pasta cook at a similar rate.

Cook and Serve Simply

Bring a large pot of water to a boil and season it with salt. Fresh pasta usually cooks much faster than dried pasta, so begin checking it after a short time; the exact cooking time depends on thickness and shape. Taste a strand to decide when it is ready, then drain it carefully.

Fresh pasta pairs beautifully with uncomplicated sauces: butter and herbs, olive oil with garlic, a slow-cooked tomato sauce, or a little grated cheese. A splash of pasta cooking water can help bring a sauce and noodles together. Finish the dish just before serving so the texture remains pleasant.

Make the Ritual Your Own

The first attempt may produce uneven ribbons or dough that needs a little extra kneading. That is part of learning. Make a note of what worked, adjust the next batch, and enjoy the chance to practise a skill that rewards attention rather than speed.

Set aside an unhurried afternoon, put on some music, and invite someone to help cut the pasta or prepare the sauce. The meal becomes more than a recipe: it becomes a small shared ritual built from simple ingredients and time well spent.
