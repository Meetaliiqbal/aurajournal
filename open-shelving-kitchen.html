Aesthetic and Functional Open Shelving

Published by Aura Journal • October 2026

Open shelving can make a kitchen feel lighter and more personal. Everyday plates, favourite mugs, glassware, and a few carefully selected objects become part of the room instead of disappearing behind cabinet doors. The arrangement can also make frequently used items easier to reach, provided the shelves are planned around real cooking habits.

Its appeal comes with a practical consideration: everything on display needs a little attention. Dust, cooking residue, and visual clutter can accumulate when shelves are overloaded or placed too close to the busiest parts of the kitchen. The most successful arrangement balances attractive display with the way the room is actually used.

Decide What Deserves to Stay Visible

Begin by identifying the items you use often. Everyday bowls, plates, mugs, and drinking glasses are natural candidates because they earn their place through regular use. Pieces used only once or twice a year may be better stored in a closed cupboard, especially if they require frequent cleaning before use.

Choose a manageable group of items rather than transferring the entire contents of your cabinets to open shelves. This creates a more deliberate arrangement and leaves room to remove or replace things without having to reorganise the whole kitchen.

“The best open shelf is one that makes daily routines easier while giving the room space to breathe.”

Plan Placement Around Kitchen Tasks

Think about where you prepare food, wash dishes, and serve meals. Shelves holding everyday plates may be convenient near a dining area, while mugs can sit near the place where you prepare tea or coffee. Keeping related items together reduces unnecessary movement through the kitchen.

Placement also needs to account for heat, steam, grease, and splashes. Avoid locating exposed dishes directly above areas where cooking residue or heavy steam will collect. Follow the shelf manufacturer's guidance for installation and load limits, and use suitable fixings for the wall type.

  • Near the dishwasher: Store dishes where they are easy to return after cleaning, provided the area is suitable and safe.
  • Near a beverage station: Keep mugs and glasses together for a simple morning routine.
  • Away from intense cooking zones: Reduce exposure to grease and heat where practical.

Create Balance with Shape and Repetition

Open shelves tend to look more cohesive when some elements repeat. This might mean a consistent colour across plates, a similar finish among mugs, or glassware grouped together. Repetition creates a visual connection without requiring every item to be identical.

Varying heights and shapes helps prevent the arrangement from looking like a rigid row of objects. Stack a few plates, line up glasses, and leave space between groups. A small ceramic bowl or a single decorative object can add interest, but accessories should not make everyday items difficult to access.

Choose Materials That Suit Your Kitchen

Wooden shelves introduce warmth and can complement timber cabinets, cutting boards, and neutral finishes. Metal shelving may suit a more industrial interior, while painted shelves can blend into the wall for a quieter effect. Consider how the shelf material relates to the worktops, cabinet hardware, and other fixed elements already in the room.

The choice should also reflect maintenance needs. Kitchen surfaces are exposed to changes in temperature, moisture, and occasional spills. Use materials and finishes suitable for the environment, and follow the manufacturer's instructions for cleaning and care.

Keep the Colour Palette Simple

A restrained colour palette can help open shelving feel intentional. White or cream ceramics, clear glass, and a few wooden elements create a neutral base that works with many kitchen styles. If your kitchen already includes strong colours, let the shelves complement those choices instead of introducing several unrelated shades.

You do not need to replace perfectly useful dishes to achieve a coordinated look. Grouping similar colours together or arranging existing pieces by function can make a noticeable difference without unnecessary spending.

Leave Enough Empty Space

It can be tempting to fill every inch of a shelf, particularly when storage is limited. However, tightly packed objects can make the entire wall feel busy and make it harder to retrieve dishes safely. Leave visible gaps between groups and make sure frequently used items can be removed without disturbing everything around them.

If you consistently run out of room, reconsider what belongs on display. A combination of open shelves and closed storage often works better than trying to make every item visible. Open shelving is a design choice, not a requirement to abandon practical cupboards.

Build a Simple Cleaning Routine

Because exposed items collect dust and kitchen residue, regular maintenance is part of keeping open shelves attractive. The amount of cleaning required depends on how often you cook, the location of the shelves, and the ventilation in your kitchen.

  • Dust routinely: Wipe shelf surfaces and exposed objects before residue builds up.
  • Wash before use when needed: Clean dishes that have been exposed to dust or cooking splashes.
  • Remove unnecessary items: Fewer objects make cleaning faster and reduce visual clutter.
  • Check the shelf structure: Inspect fittings periodically and follow the installation guidance for safe use.

Make the Arrangement Work for Your Household

There is no single ideal open-shelving layout. A household that cooks frequently may need easy access to a broad selection of everyday dishes, while a smaller kitchen may benefit from a narrow display of only the most-used pieces. Children, pets, and the height of household members can also influence where items should be stored.

Keep fragile or heavy objects in locations where they can be handled safely. Do not place items above seating or food-preparation areas unless the shelving and installation are appropriate for that use. When in doubt, prioritise secure storage over appearance.

Open shelving works best when it reflects a real routine rather than a staged photograph. Choose useful items, group them thoughtfully, leave space around them, and maintain the shelves regularly. With that balance, your kitchen can feel both practical and visually considered.
