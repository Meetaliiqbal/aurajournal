Garment Care: Protecting Wool and Cashmere

Published by Aura Journal • August 2026

Wool and cashmere are valued for their warmth, softness, and ability to bring texture to a wardrobe. A well-chosen jumper, cardigan, or scarf can become a dependable part of cold-weather dressing. Yet natural-fibre knitwear often needs a different approach to care than everyday cotton clothing.

The most important habit is to avoid treating every garment in the same way. Fibre composition, knit structure, dyes, and finishing methods can influence how an item responds to water, heat, and handling. Reading the care label before washing is the safest starting point.

Understand the Material First

Wool fibres naturally provide insulation and can help regulate warmth. Cashmere, which comes from cashmere goats, is prized for its fine, soft feel. Both materials can be made into lightweight or substantial knitwear, and their care needs may vary depending on the finished garment.

Some items are suitable for gentle hand washing, while others require professional dry cleaning. A garment labelled for dry cleaning should not automatically be washed at home, even if another wool jumper you own can tolerate water. Follow the instructions provided for the individual piece.

"Good garment care is less about doing more and more about using the right method for the material in front of you."

Wash Gently and Only When Needed

Knitwear does not always need to be washed after every wear. Depending on the garment and how it has been used, airing it between wears may be sufficient. Deal with stains promptly using a method appropriate to the fibre, rather than repeatedly washing an item without need.

When the care label permits hand washing, use cool or lukewarm water and a suitable wool detergent. Avoid vigorous rubbing, twisting, or sudden temperature changes, which may contribute to felting, distortion, or shrinkage. Rinse gently if the detergent instructions require it.

  • Check the label: Confirm whether hand washing, machine washing, or professional cleaning is recommended.
  • Use a suitable detergent: Choose a product designed for wool or delicate fibres when appropriate.
  • Handle carefully: Avoid stretching, scrubbing, or twisting the knitwear.
  • Separate thoughtfully: Keep similar colors together and take extra care with garments that may release dye.

Dry Without Stretching the Knit

Drying is an important part of knitwear care because wet fibres can be more vulnerable to stretching and distortion. After washing, support the garment rather than lifting it by one edge. Gently press out excess water without wringing it.

Many wool and cashmere garments are best dried flat on a clean, dry towel, reshaped to their natural dimensions. Keep them away from direct heat sources and strong sunlight. Avoid hanging a heavy, wet jumper, as its own weight may pull the fabric out of shape.

Manage Pilling Carefully

Small fibre balls, known as pills, can develop where fabric rubs against itself or another surface. They commonly appear around the underarms, sides, cuffs, and areas where bags or seat belts create friction. Pilling does not necessarily mean that a garment is poorly made; it can occur with many soft fibres and knitted structures.

If pills appear, use a suitable fabric comb or knitwear tool according to its instructions. Work gently on a flat surface and avoid pulling pills off by hand, as doing so can damage the surrounding fibres. Test any tool on a discreet area first.

Store Knitwear in the Right Way

Fold wool and cashmere jumpers rather than hanging them on narrow hangers that may stretch the shoulders. Store them in a clean, dry place, and make sure garments are fully dry before putting them away for an extended period.

For seasonal storage, use a breathable storage container or bag appropriate for the environment. Keep the storage area clean, and check garments occasionally for signs of moisture or pests. Cedar products may be used as part of a storage routine, but they should not replace cleaning and checking clothing.

Protect Against Friction and Everyday Wear

The way you wear a garment can affect how it ages. Repeated rubbing from shoulder bags, rough outer layers, and tight seat belts may increase surface pilling. This does not mean you need to avoid wearing favourite pieces; simply notice where friction occurs and give delicate knitwear appropriate attention.

Allowing a garment to rest between wears can also help it recover its shape. If a jumper becomes creased, follow the manufacturer's advice on steaming or pressing instead of applying direct heat without checking the fabric requirements.

Repair Small Problems Early

Loose threads, small holes, and weakened seams are often easier to address before they become larger problems. Avoid pulling loose yarns, which can distort the knit. If you are uncertain how to repair a delicate garment, a professional repair service may be the more suitable option.

Thoughtful care helps you get more use from the clothes you already own. By following the label, washing gently, drying carefully, and storing knitwear properly, you can help preserve the comfort and appearance that made wool and cashmere appealing in the first place.
